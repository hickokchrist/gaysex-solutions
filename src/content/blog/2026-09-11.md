<h2>It was, like, my second week of high school</h2>
<p>
I woke up alone to get ready for school as usual. My mother was already at work by that time. Turning on the TV, I liked to wake up with the anchors on the Eugene news affiliates. Immediately it was obvious something catastrophic happened, there was a single burning building being shown on every channel. Every single one. My immediate thought was that it must be the anniversary of some kind of attack (lmao!)
</p>
<p>
The buildings weren't necessarily anything I could have named before the day, it turned out I came to be aware of the situation after the first tower already collapsed. My memory isn't too clear, but I think I saw the second tower collapse live on TV. It was difficult to parse the situation in the ~30 minutes or so of news coverage I was glued to. I remember writing a note to my mother for after she came home from work about the planes hitting the buildings, and the pentagon, and at the time we thoguht there was a car bomb outside the state department. What was that about? Also, later on, what was with the anthrax?
</p>
<p>
I walked to school and got breakfast, a bagel with cream cheese. I chatted with my friend Chris about what happened, because it was the most insane thing I'd ever witnessed and at that point I hadn't seen a single human being acknowledge the situation. He had seen it on TV too, we didn't know what to make of it all. Chris ended up drowning almost exactly three years later, but that was unrelated to 9/11.
</p>
<p>
First period was Social Studies. How appropriate. Mr. "Riddle" simply turned on our classrom TV affixed to the upper corner of every classroom and turned on the news and started talking. It was his first year teaching at our school, possibly anywhere. I believe he is still teaching there, or he was last time I trawled the staff roster. I remember the teacher responding to some snickering teen "guess what! We're at WAR now". My mother said the same thing later that night, about being at war. Our elders were concerned for the onslought our young bodies would be carrying out.
</p>
<p>
Every morning during school days, the school would put on "Channel One", which, looking back, was pure state propaganda. I remember hearing about the "Northern Alliance" of native Afghani militia fighting with the US Army against the Taliban. 
</p>
<p>
I remember when Iraq was invaded in 2003. Everyone asked, "why?" Unfortunately, it set the modern precedent for wildly unjustified deployment of the United States military. Looking back, it was all just an elaborate shell game to consolidate power to lead us where we are now.
</p>
